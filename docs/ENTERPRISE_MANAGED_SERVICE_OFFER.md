# OpsPilot Managed Enterprise Automation Offer

**Commercial target:** USD 2,000 per customer per month  
**Delivery model:** Hybrid software subscription + managed automation service  
**Status:** Proposed offer for customer discovery and negotiation; not a published price list or contractual SLA.

## 1. Positioning

OpsPilot helps operations teams automate and monitor repeatable business processes. The offer combines the OpsPilot platform with implementation, workflow configuration, integration support, exception monitoring, and monthly value reviews.

Sell an operational outcome and accountable service, not access to a generic collection of automation utilities.

**Core promise:** We identify a high-volume operational process, implement a controlled automation workflow, monitor its performance, and report measured outcomes.

Do not guarantee a fixed percentage of savings, error reduction, or uptime before a customer's baseline, process constraints, and deployment environment are assessed.

## 2. Ideal first customer

Prioritize organizations that have:
- A recurring, document- or spreadsheet-heavy process.
- Enough monthly volume to justify implementation and managed support.
- A named process owner who can approve rules and exceptions.
- An accessible test environment or synthetic/sample data.
- A clear business impact from delay, rekeying, manual review, or inconsistent processing.
- Authority to approve the required integrations and data handling.

Good discovery candidates include customer onboarding operations, invoice intake, employee record processing, claims/document intake, and recurring reconciliation preparation. Regulated or sensitive processes require additional security, privacy, legal and domain review before production use.

Avoid promising a fit solely because a prospect has a large employee count. Process volume, exception rate, integration access, risk, and measurable value are stronger qualification criteria.

## 3. Proposed $2,000/month package

### Included platform access
- One customer organization/workspace once multi-tenant controls are implemented.
- Agreed number of production workflows, initially target up to 3.
- Workflow configuration and version management.
- Execution history, failure visibility, and downloadable reports.
- Agreed integrations and usage allowance defined in the order form.
- Named customer users and role-based access once identity and authorization are released.

### Included managed service
- Initial process discovery and automation suitability assessment.
- Workflow configuration and test support.
- Agreed business-hours monitoring of in-scope workflow executions.
- Exception triage and incident communication.
- Monthly performance and value review.
- A defined monthly change allowance, proposed up to 4 hours, for minor rule/configuration changes.
- Documentation and handover for agreed workflows.

### Explicit exclusions unless separately quoted
- Unlimited workflows, changes, or support hours.
- 24/7 staffed monitoring or emergency response.
- Major process redesign outside the agreed scope.
- Bespoke connectors or vendor fees.
- Customer source-system licensing and third-party API charges.
- Legal, tax, accounting, HR or regulated decision-making.
- Guaranteed financial savings, zero errors, or a compliance certification.
- Large historical-data cleanup or one-off migration projects.

The final contract must specify exact workflow count, volume limits, integrations, support hours, response targets, data retention, hosting region, and overage/change rates.

## 4. Implementation and onboarding

### Stage 0 - Qualification (before contract)
- Interview process owner and operators.
- Map current process and exception paths.
- Establish monthly volume, handling time, rework and turnaround baseline.
- Identify source systems, data types, access, retention and risk.
- Estimate achievable value and technical complexity.
- Produce a written scope, exclusions, assumptions and acceptance tests.

### Stage 1 - Paid implementation
Recommended one-time setup fee: quote separately based on complexity. Do not hide substantial integration or migration effort inside the monthly subscription.

Deliver:
- Agreed process map and workflow specification.
- Configured workflow(s) and input schema.
- Test plan using customer-approved synthetic or masked data.
- Exception and rollback procedure.
- User acceptance testing and sign-off.
- Operator documentation and support contacts.

### Stage 2 - Controlled go-live
- Start with a limited volume or parallel-run period.
- Keep a human approval step for material or irreversible actions.
- Reconcile output against the source system.
- Record defects, exceptions and manual overrides.
- Expand volume only after acceptance criteria pass.

### Stage 3 - Managed monthly operation
- Monitor agreed executions and exception queues.
- Report measured activity separately from estimates.
- Review performance, cost and improvement backlog.
- Approve changes through a documented process.
- Reconfirm scope and value at renewal.

## 5. Service levels to negotiate

These are proposed business-hours targets, not an existing production capability or contractual commitment. Final response windows must match actual staffing and hosting arrangements.

| Severity | Example | Proposed initial response target |
|---|---|---|
| P1 Critical | In-scope production process unavailable with material business impact | 4 business hours |
| P2 High | Major workflow degraded; no reasonable workaround | 1 business day |
| P3 Normal | Individual execution issue with workaround | 2 business days |
| P4 Request | Minor change, question, or enhancement | 3 business days |

Define support hours, time zone, holidays, incident communication channel, customer dependencies, and what constitutes an incident versus a change request in the contract. Response targets are not resolution guarantees.

## 6. Value measurement

Agree a baseline with the customer before go-live. Capture:
- Eligible monthly case/record volume.
- Baseline manual handling minutes per record.
- Actual human review and exception-handling minutes after automation.
- Customer-approved loaded labor cost per hour.
- Rework/error rate where the customer can reliably measure it.
- Workflow failures, retries, skips and manual overrides.
- Third-party and hosting usage costs.

A transparent estimate:

`Estimated hours saved = eligible volume × (baseline manual minutes - post-automation human minutes) / 60`

`Estimated gross labor value = estimated hours saved × customer-approved hourly labor cost`

`Estimated net value = gross labor value - subscription - setup allocation - usage and support costs`

If post-automation human time is unknown, do not assume it is zero. Mark savings unavailable or show a clearly labelled scenario. Separate measured execution metrics from customer-supplied assumptions and modelled estimates.

## 7. Customer responsibilities

The customer must:
- Appoint a process owner and technical contact.
- Approve process rules, field mappings, exceptions and acceptance criteria.
- Provide lawful access to systems and data.
- Confirm data classification, legal basis, retention, residency and vendor approvals.
- Provide test data and review test outputs.
- Approve go-live and material changes.
- Maintain source-system availability and valid credentials.
- Review exceptions and make decisions reserved for qualified staff.

OpsPilot must not independently make regulated or high-impact decisions on behalf of the customer.

## 8. Security and data handling commitments

Do not sell shared hosted access to confidential customer data until tenant isolation, authentication, authorization, secure file handling, secret management, audit logs, backups, incident response and appropriate contractual privacy terms have been implemented and reviewed.

Before onboarding:
- Document data flows, subprocessors and hosting region.
- Minimize fields collected and retained.
- Agree retention and deletion windows.
- Define encryption and access controls.
- Agree incident notification responsibilities.
- Test backup restoration and access revocation.
- Obtain appropriate legal/privacy review for the customer's jurisdiction and sector.

Do not claim SOC 2, ISO 27001, GDPR compliance or equivalent certification without evidence and qualified review.

## 9. Commercial qualification and pricing guardrails

A $2,000 monthly fee is justified only where the customer's measured or credible expected value, risk reduction, capacity release, or service requirement supports it.

Before proposing the fee, document:
- Business problem and accountable owner.
- Current monthly process cost or operational impact.
- Expected value and confidence level.
- Implementation effort and third-party costs.
- Ongoing monitoring and support burden.
- Security and integration complexity.
- Agreed success criteria and review date.

If the value case is weak, narrow the scope, propose a paid discovery/implementation engagement, or decline the opportunity. Do not discount by silently accepting unlimited support or bespoke work.

## 10. First-customer success criteria

A first customer should not be considered successful merely because a workflow runs once. Require:
- Customer-approved process map and baseline.
- Accepted workflow and test evidence.
- Reconciled output over an agreed observation period.
- Documented exception handling and rollback.
- Named trained process owner.
- Monthly operational report with source metrics.
- Support workload within the agreed allowance.
- Customer confirmation that the outcome justifies renewal.
